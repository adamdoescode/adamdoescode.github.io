> **Preamble:** This piece is pretty drafty... you should treat it as a loose pile of ideas motivating why I am posting anything at all to a blog. Its probably no more coherent than a frontier clanker. But it was written entirely by a human.
{: .notice }

This is a brief note to summarise the extent of LLM help for this blog. **In short, a human (me!) writes these posts**. But I am more than happy to use LLMs and other tools to handle infrastructure, research, and even help with proofreading.

Things I might use an LLM for:
- proofreading (but NOT re-writing).
- formatting (Claude helped with the notice block at the top!).
- rephrasing a sentence (with great caution and trepidation!).
- site code (Claude and ChatGPT were immensely helpful for the infrastructure to get this blog onto my GitHub Pages).

Things I wont use an LLM for:
- the actual words in my posts.

The benefits I see in blogging are that:
- writing is a way to encode/offload thoughts.
- publishing your thoughts is a way to contribute your ideas.

There have been [many](https://writethatblog.substack.com/p/dev-reaction-to-ai-blog-posts) [very](https://sockpuppet.org/blog/2026/09/17/how-to-write-with-an-llm/) [good](https://alexhwoods.com/dont-let-ai-write-for-you/) articles written about why letting an LLM write for you is problematic, so I won't repeat that here, except to say that I agree that it's a bad idea to let an LLM write for you. Bots are not aligned to you, they are aligned to whoever trained them, at least until we get [Guardian Angels](https://gwern.net/guardian-angel).

With the long and painful demise of Reddit without any good replacement I have turned more and more to books and blogs via RSS for my reading diet. I see a blog as a way to contribute a bit to the RSS network, and there's not much point doing that if it's Dario and Sam's voice and not my own!
